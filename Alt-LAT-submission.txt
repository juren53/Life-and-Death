Subject: Op-Ed Submission: The Heroic Choice of Major Jack Hennessy (60th Anniversary)

Dear Editor,

On September 17, 1966, a Navy bomber converted into a wildfire tanker went down in flames just one block from my childhood front door in North Hollywood. As his aircraft failed, the pilot—Major John "Jack" Hennessy, a decorated veteran of World War II and Korea—turned away from the neighborhood below. He sacrificed his own life to save the families on the ground.

I was there, and I saw it happen.

With the 60th anniversary of the crash approaching on September 17, 2026, I have written a personal essay titled "Learning the Meaning of Life and Death." It reflects on what I witnessed that afternoon, my research into Hennessy’s life in the decades since, and how his ultimate sacrifice taught me to distinguish between everyday worries and what truly matters.

As an LA native and retired pilot who trained at Burbank Airport—the same field Hennessy took off from that day—I have carried this story for six decades. My op-eds have previously appeared in the Kansas City Star. Given its deep roots in Los Angeles history and the upcoming milestone, I am submitting this piece for consideration as a featured Op-Ed.
